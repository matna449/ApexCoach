# 30-day HRV rolling average: window excludes today, 7-day minimum sample before it's trusted, cold-start forces a NEUTRAL band via a delta of 0

docs/adr/0013 deferred computing `hrv_30d_avg_ms` entirely — `classify_daily_inputs()` takes it as a plain parameter and stays pure, leaving "30-day window semantics, whether today's own reading counts, what happens with fewer than 30 days of history" as an explicit, undecided gap. F11.4 (#44) closes that gap: `apex_coach/services/hrv_trend.py` reads `MetricsRepository.get_daily_metrics_range()` and produces the number `classify_daily_inputs()` expects. Two decisions were needed, not just plumbing.

**The trailing window for date D is the 30 calendar days strictly before D — `[D-30, D-1]` — never including D itself.** `compute_hrv_30d_avg(repo, date)` computes `end = date - 1 day`, `start = end - 29 days`, and reads that inclusive range. The alternative — including D's own row, which may already exist by the time classification runs (the WHOOP fetch for today happens before classification in the morning flow) — would mean today's HRV partly forms its own baseline, biasing the average toward whatever today happens to be and muting the very delta the baseline exists to surface. A baseline must be prior history, not a mix of prior history and the value being measured against it.

**`compute_hrv_30d_avg()` itself makes no cold-start decision — it averages over however many valid readings actually exist in the window (skipping rows where `whoop_hrv_ms is None`, per ADR-0012's upsert model — a health-check-only day carries no WHOOP reading) and returns `(None, 0)` when there are zero.** It returns `(average, sample_count)`, not just a float, so callers can see how much history backed the number. This satisfies the acceptance criteria literally: full 30 days averages 30 values, 5 days of history averages those 5, zero history returns `None`.

**The cold-start policy — what to *do* with a low-confidence average — lives in a separate function, `resolve_hrv_30d_avg_for_classification(repo, date, current_hrv_ms, min_sample_days=7)`, which is what actually feeds `classify_daily_inputs()`.** Below `MIN_SAMPLE_DAYS` (7) valid readings, the "average" is 1-6 noisy data points, not a trend — trusting it risks classifying a spurious POSITIVE/NEGATIVE/STRONG_NEG band from statistical noise in someone's first week of real use, exactly the failure mode a cold-start policy needs to prevent. Below that threshold, the function returns `current_hrv_ms` itself as the "baseline." Fed into `classify_daily_inputs()`, that makes `hrv_delta_ms = hrv_ms - hrv_30d_avg_ms == 0` exactly, which `classify_hrv_delta()` bands as `HRV_NEUTRAL` (its inclusive `-5..+5` range, per docs/adr/0013) — an explicit no-signal outcome. 7 was chosen as roughly "a week's worth of data," long enough to smooth out single bad-sleep-night outliers while still reaching a real (non-fallback) baseline well before the 30-day window fills — `min_sample_days` is a keyword parameter, not hardcoded, if that number needs revisiting later.

**This needed no change to `classify_daily_inputs()`'s signature.** The ticket flagged this as a possible outcome of choosing a "NEUTRAL/no-signal" policy — bypassing `classify_hrv_delta` for a cold-start case would need a signature change (e.g. an `Optional[float]` or a sentinel). Forcing the delta to exactly 0 via the fallback baseline achieves the identical classified outcome (`HRV_NEUTRAL`) through the existing parameter, so `classify_daily_inputs()` and `classify_hrv_delta()` are untouched — the cold-start decision is fully contained in `hrv_trend.py`. The tradeoff: `result["hrv_delta_ms"]` will read `0.0` for a cold-start day even though "we didn't have enough data" and "HRV was exactly at baseline" are different facts. Nothing downstream currently branches on `hrv_delta_ms` for anything but banding (this ticket didn't need to touch daily_engine or the CLI, and neither did ADR-0013's own scope), so the two cases being indistinguishable at that field costs nothing today; if a future ticket needs to tell them apart (e.g. to render "not enough history yet" instead of "HRV neutral" in CLI output), the caller can check `compute_hrv_30d_avg()`'s own `sample_count` directly rather than relying on the parameter value classify_daily_inputs sees.

**Alternative considered:** average over whatever's available with no minimum at all, even a single day. Rejected as the cold-start default — a 1-day "average" is just yesterday's HRV, and comparing today against it is really comparing today against yesterday, a much noisier signal than the 30-day rolling baseline the classification bands (§2.2, ±5/±10 ms) were tuned against. Requiring a minimum before trusting the average, with an explicit no-signal fallback below it, matches the spirit of "fewer than 30 days is every day for the first month of real use" — it accepts a degraded (NEUTRAL) signal during that month rather than a misleading one.
